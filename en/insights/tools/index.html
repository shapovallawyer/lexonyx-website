Tools

Practical tools for initial assessment of an international structure: tax risks, PE-risk, VAT, banking readiness and regulatory alignment.

Tools help determine the level of risk and identify zones that require structural analysis. They do not replace a personalised review and are not a ready-made solution.

Group structure · Governance

Checklist on cross-border structuring

  • Does the structure correspond to the allocation of functions?
  • Where are key management decisions made?
  • Is the economic logic of the group documented?
  • Is there alignment between the tax model and governance system?

Checklist on PE-risk and people footprint

FAQ

Briefly, to the point

What are LEXONYX tools?

Tools are practical checklists for the initial assessment of an international structure: tax risks, PE-risk, VAT, banking readiness and regulatory alignment. They help quickly identify which zones of the model require attention and prepare for a deeper review by structuring self-assessment around the key elements.

Which checklists are available?

Available checklists cover cross-border structuring, PE-risk and people footprint, VAT and fixed establishment, and banking readiness. Each checklist covers its own element of the system and helps understand how aligned the model is in that area and where gaps may exist between the structure and real activity.

How should the checklists be used?

A checklist is a structured set of self-assessment questions. By going through it, you can see which zones of the model raise doubts and require attention. This is initial diagnostics: it does not replace analysis, but helps clarify priorities and prepare meaningful questions for a structural review.

Is a checklist enough instead of analysis?

No. A checklist provides an initial picture and helps structure self-assessment, but it does not evaluate the interconnections between the elements of the model. Real risks often arise at the interfaces: tax residency affects PE-risk, which affects VAT and the bank. Only personalised analysis of the structure as a system provides the full picture.

NEXT STEP

Is structural analysis required?

International models require a systemic assessment. We analyse tax, governance and banking logic in combination, not separately.
