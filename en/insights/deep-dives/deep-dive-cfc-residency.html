CFC and the Founder’s Tax Residency
Why a structure ≠ personal protection

How an international company, personal tax residency and CFC rules interact. Where personal risk arises — even in a “correct” structure.

The Main Illusion

It is often assumed that if a company is incorporated in another country, its profit is “outside the reach” of personal tax.

In modern tax architecture, profit follows control.

Tax Residency of the Individual

Most countries apply criteria such as:

  • Centre of vital interests
  • Actual residence
  • Family and economic activity
  • The 183-day rule

If you are tax resident, worldwide income may be reportable.

CFC — logic of the rules

Controlled Foreign Company rules are designed to prevent profit being shifted to low-tax jurisdictions.

  • Control (usually >50% or de facto)
  • Low level of taxation
  • Passive income

Even undistributed profit may be included in the individual tax base.

Typical Risk Scenarios

1. Founder in the EU — company outside the EU

If the founder is resident in a country with a CFC regime, the foreign company’s profit may be taxed at the founder level.

2. HoldCo in a “convenient” jurisdiction

A low rate does not, by itself, eliminate CFC risks.

3. Passive IP income

Passive royalties are often a CFC trigger.

Where misalignment arises

  • The company is profitable, but no dividends are paid
  • The founder manages from a country with a strict CFC regime
  • The structure was built without regard to personal tax
  • There is no coordination between corporate and personal planning

Strategic Approach

An international structure must be designed on two levels at the same time:

  • Corporate architecture
  • Personal tax positioning

It is not possible to assess the robustness of a structure while ignoring personal tax residency.

We will assess it from both the company perspective and the personal tax-risk perspective.
