FLAGSHIP EXPERTISE

Group Structuring

A group structure is not a set of companies but an aligned system: functions, assets, risks and governance must fit together with the tax logic and with the bank.

Request a review See the audit format
Functions · Assets · Risks · Governance
WHY THIS MATTERS

Even a single company already raises structural questions

Even if the business today has one company in one country, structuring questions already exist: who takes decisions, where economic value is actually created, where the people and functions are, whether there is a permanent establishment risk, whether the owner has CFC exposure, and how the model will be read by a bank, an investor and a tax authority.

The jurisdiction does not decide everything

A strong country does not rescue weak architecture if management, people and assets are distributed without logic.

Flows must match functions

If profit ends up where there is no role, control or actual presence, the model quickly becomes vulnerable.

External review tests the whole system

A bank, an investor and local counsel look at the coherence of the model, not only at registration documents.

TYPICAL SITUATIONS

When group structuring becomes the task

Launching a new international group

You need a structure built around a specific business model, not a standard "by country" scheme.

Rebuilding an existing structure

The current configuration creates tax, banking or corporate conflicts.

Moving functions or assets

People, IP, company roles, the contractual chain and the profit allocation logic all change.

Preparing for a bank, a deal or an investor

The structure must withstand review for explainability, transparency and feasibility.

Entering the EU / UK / UAE

You need a workable architecture for a new market that accounts for the local tax and regulatory context.

A team growing across several countries

Questions arise on PE-risk, actual presence, CFC, VAT and governance.

CONFIGURATIONS

The models we work with

These are not "ready-made schemes" but working types of configuration. They are adapted to the actual operating model, the owners' tax residency, banking requirements and regulatory constraints.

01

Holding and operating company

A holding company owns the operating entities and concentrates ownership, capital and key assets.

We check: actual presence, management and control, withholding taxes, CFC risks and treaty access.
02

Service company within the group

A separate company provides services within the group: management, development, marketing, back-office or support functions.

We check: arm's length pricing, the economic role of the service company, PE-risk and documentation.
03

IP structure and licensing

A company owns the intellectual property and licenses it to the operating entities.

We check: IP development and management functions, withholding on royalties, the reality of the IP company's role and the limits set by anti-abuse rules.
04

Finance or treasury company

A separate entity manages intra-group financing, liquidity and loans.

We check: rates, business purpose, the explainability of the model, withholding on interest and banking readability.
05

Direct ownership by the founder

The owner holds several companies in different jurisdictions directly, without a separate holding.

We check: personal residency, CFC, the governance logic, exit from the structure and future scalability.
06

Regional expansion

The group enters the EU, the United Kingdom or the UAE through regional entities with a new operating and regulatory logic.

We check: the regulatory perimeter, presence requirements, VAT logic, banking expectations and the distribution of teams.
WHAT WE ANALYSE

The elements that make up the structural analysis

Corporate architecture and ownership

Who owns the companies, how roles are allocated and how the whole group chain is arranged.

Functions, assets and risks

Where income is actually generated, who bears the entrepreneurial risk and where the key assets sit.

Governance and control

Who takes decisions, where this happens and whether formal governance matches the reality of the business.

Tax logic, treaties and anti-abuse rules

How the structure relates to the owners' residency and control rules: access to double tax treaties (DTT), the withholding tax (WHT) position, beneficial ownership, the principal purpose test (PPT) as modified by the MLI, general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and the allocation of profit across functions, assets and risks (transfer pricing).

Banking readability

How explainable the model is in terms of the group, its flows, documents and the source of funds.

Regulatory environment

Whether the model itself changes the status of the product, the need for a licence or the requirements for how operations are structured.

In essence, we stitch the corporate architecture together with the tax logic, actual management and the expectations of a bank or investor — so that the model is defensible, workable and scalable.

TYPICAL RISKS

Where structures most often break

The formal role of a company does not match reality

A company is described as a holding, service or IP centre but does not perform that function in practice.

Profit is allocated without economic logic

Income is concentrated where there is no control, team, function or demonstrable business purpose.

People and management are in another country

Questions arise on residency, CFC, PE-risk and effective management.

The structure does not withstand banking review

Flows, ownership, documents and the explanation of the model do not look coherent to an external counterparty.

The regulatory perimeter is not built into the architecture

New activity requires different company roles, a licence or a rebuild of the whole operating chain.

No headroom for growth

The model works only at the current scale and breaks when the team, markets or product expand.

WHAT TO PREPARE

What is usually needed for an initial review

A list of the group's companies, ownership and the roles of the participants
Where the owners, directors, team and key functions are located
How the contractual and cash flows are arranged
What assets the group holds: IP, brands, licences, contracts, client base
Which banks, payment solutions or investors are already involved
What changes are planned: a new country, a new company, product, deal or licence

A perfect document pack is not needed at the first stage. What is needed is a clear map of reality, so that we can determine the scale of the task and choose the right format of work.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Group structuring — in short

What is group structuring?

Group structuring is the aligned allocation of functions, assets, risks and governance across the companies of a group, so that the corporate architecture matches the tax logic, actual presence, banking expectations and the regulatory environment. It is a working model of the business, not a scheme on paper, and external review tests it as such.

Is structuring needed if the business has only one company?

Yes. Even with one company in one country, structural questions arise: who takes decisions, where economic value is created, where the people and functions are, whether there is a permanent establishment risk and whether the owner has CFC exposure. These questions determine how the model will be read by a bank, an investor and a tax authority.

What typical group configurations exist?

The main working types are: a holding and operating company, a service company within the group, an IP structure with licensing, a finance or treasury company, direct ownership of several companies by the founder, and regional expansion into the EU, the United Kingdom or the UAE. Each configuration is adapted to the actual operating model and its constraints.

Where do group structures most often break?

Most often a structure breaks when the formal role of a company does not match reality, when profit is allocated without economic logic, when people and management sit in another country, when the model does not withstand banking review, when the regulatory perimeter is not built into the architecture, or when there is no headroom for growth as teams and markets expand.

What is analysed in a structural review of a group?

The analysis covers corporate architecture and ownership, the allocation of functions, assets and risks, governance and control, tax logic and CFC, treaty access and anti-abuse rules, the banking readability of flows and the source of funds, and the regulatory environment. The aim is to stitch these elements into a defensible, workable and scalable model.

What should be prepared for an initial structural review?

For an initial review a clear map of reality is enough: a list of the group's companies and ownership, where the owners, directors and team are located, how the contractual and cash flows work, what assets the group holds, which banks and providers are involved and what changes are planned. A perfect document pack is not required at the start.
