REGULATORY ARCHITECTURE

Regulatory Architecture and Licensing

Licensing is a strategic choice, not an application form: it depends on the model, jurisdiction, capital and MiCA / PSD3 / AMLR.

Request a Regulatory Review Return to Group Structuring
EMI / PI / CASP · Regulatory scoping · Jurisdiction choice · Compliance framework
WHY THIS MATTERS

The wrong licensing path can break a business before it scales

The core issue is not only whether a licence is obtained. It is the initial misclassification of the model, the wrong jurisdiction choice, underestimating MiCA / PSD3 / AMLR and building a weak compliance framework.

A licence is a strategic choice

It affects capital requirements, banking access, passporting, operational design and investment appeal.

Not every model needs a full licence from the outset

Sometimes scoping must come first: licence, exemption, agent/distributor model or staged approach.

The regulatory landscape is changing

MiCA, PSD3 and AMLR are changing licensing logic, transitional periods and requirements for future projects.

REGULATORY CONTEXT

The regulatory landscape for 2026–2028

MiCA, PSD3 and AMLR cannot be treated as background. They already shape model design at the outset.

MiCA

MiCA replaces national VASP registrations with a single CASP authorisation and EU passporting, including transition deadlines and separate capital classes for CASPs.

PSD3 / PSR

PSD3 is a future driver of the convergence of EMI and PI into a unified payment institution logic, with a re-authorisation window for existing licences.

AMLR

AMLR strengthens direct AML/KYC regulation and makes the compliance framework an even more central component of licensing architecture.

Licensing can no longer be treated as a local task. It is architecture designed for pan-European regulation, intensified supervision and subsequent scaling.

REGIME TYPES

Which regulatory paths are usually considered

EMI, PI, CASP and lighter entry routes are regulatory architecture options, not a product catalogue.

EMI

Electronic Money Institution: e-money issuance and payment services. Capital from €350,000 and a broader operational scope.

PI

Payment Institution: payment services without e-money issuance; capital of €20k–125k and usually a faster process than an EMI.

CASP / MiCA

Crypto-asset service provider under MiCA: exchange, custody, wallet and related activities; capital bands of €50k / €125k / €150k depending on the class of services.

Exemptions / staged entry

Limited network, small EMI, agent/distributor model and staged approach are possible entry routes for certain models.

WHERE ERRORS USUALLY ARISE

Typical risks of incorrect licensing

The model is classified as “technology”, although it is in fact providing regulated services
A jurisdiction is selected that fits poorly with banking needs or substance expectations
The compliance framework is assembled as a template, rather than embedded into the real operating model
The project does not account for MiCA / PSD3 / AMLR and requires restructuring within 1–2 years
Passporting, capital logic and governance requirements were underestimated at the outset
The licensing path is not aligned with the group structure, ownership and commercial model

JURISDICTIONAL LOGIC

How jurisdictions are usually compared

Lithuania, Cyprus, the UK and the UAE are common options with different passporting, timing and positioning logic.

EU

The key value is passporting. Lithuania is a faster practical route; Cyprus is a longer, but more premium-oriented path.

United Kingdom

After Brexit, FCA authorisation stands apart from EU passporting; the UK is therefore a standalone strategic choice, not an “addition to the EU”.

UAE

The UAE is a separate licensing environment without EU passporting, but with its own regulatory logic and positioning.

WHAT TO PREPARE

What is usually needed for initial regulatory scoping

A concise description of the business model and what the product actually does
Which services are planned: payments, e-money, custody, exchange, wallet, cards, and so on
Which markets and countries are planned as target markets
Whether there is already a group structure, banking setup and expected capital model
Who the founders and key managers are, and where they are located
Which timelines, budget expectations and business constraints are critical at the outset

At the first stage, what is needed is not a package draft for the regulator, but a candid map of the model and its regulatory perimeter.

FAQ

Licensing — the essentials

What is regulatory architecture, and why is it needed?

Regulatory architecture is the alignment of the business model with applicable regulation: which activities are in fact regulated, which licence is required, and in which jurisdiction. A licence is a strategic choice: it affects capital requirements, banking access, passporting, operational design and the investment appeal of the model for years ahead.

Does a business always need a full licence?

No. Not every model requires a full licence from the outset. Sometimes the correct path is regulatory scoping and lighter entry routes: exemption, limited network, small EMI, agent/distributor model or staged approach. The reverse error is also dangerous: a model is classified as technological, although it is in fact providing regulated services.

How do EMI, PI and CASP licences differ?

EMI — Electronic Money Institution: e-money issuance and payment services, capital from €350,000 and a broad operational scope. PI — Payment Institution: payment services without e-money issuance, capital of €20,000–125,000 and usually a faster process. CASP under MiCA — crypto-asset services: exchange, custody, wallet, with capital bands of €50k/€125k/€150k depending on the class of services.

How do MiCA, PSD3 and AMLR affect licensing?

MiCA replaces national VASP registrations with a single CASP authorisation and EU-wide passporting. PSD3 moves towards bringing EMI and PI into a unified payment institution logic, with a re-authorisation window for existing licences. AMLR strengthens direct AML/KYC regulation and makes the compliance framework a central component of the architecture, not an add-on to it.

How is the licensing jurisdiction selected?

The comparison turns on where the licensing path is realistic for this specific model: banking, capital, timing and target markets. In the EU, the key value is passporting: Lithuania is usually faster as a practical route; Cyprus is longer, but more premium-oriented. After Brexit, the United Kingdom is a standalone choice with separate FCA authorisation. The UAE is a separate environment without EU passporting and with its own logic.

What should be prepared for initial regulatory scoping?

A candid map of the model is enough: a concise description of the product and business model, planned services — payments, e-money, custody, exchange, wallet, cards — target markets, existing group structure and banking setup, expected capital model, who the founders and key managers are and where they are located, and the critical timing and constraints. A regulator submission package is not needed at the outset.
