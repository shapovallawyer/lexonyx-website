APPLICABILITY CONTEXT

When the Netherlands Is Genuinely Relevant

The Netherlands is not a universal solution. In specific scenarios, however, it can fit well into the logic of an international structure. Below are the contexts in which this most often occurs.

The Netherlands may also be relevant for a business operating in a single jurisdiction if the operating model, clients and management decisions are genuinely connected with the NL. Even without a group structure, it is important to assess tax residency, governance and the actual place where decisions are made.

Holding or Intermediate Company in a Real Group

The Netherlands is considered for a holding role in structures where the participation exemption, treaty network and EU legal environment are genuinely needed for the group model. The key condition is real substance and business logic, not merely legal form.

Operating Company with Genuine EU Activity

Where a company genuinely operates in the EU and its people, functions and decision-making are present in the Netherlands, a Dutch operating company may be an organic choice.

Coordination Role in a Group with Real Functions and Substance

Coordination or treasury functions within a group, where there are real people, real operations and governance in the Netherlands — not merely a registered address.

VAT and EU-facing Operating Structures

For models with EU VAT obligations, cross-border EU sales or EU warehouse / fulfilment arrangements, a Dutch operating base may simplify VAT compliance where there is real EU presence.

Investor, Bank and Counterparty Trust in Specific Contexts

The Netherlands has a strong reputation as a business jurisdiction: tier-1 banking, an EU regulatory framework and a recognised legal system. In some scenarios this has practical value — but it does not replace structural logic.

Treaty Access — Only with Real Substance and Business Justification

The Netherlands’ broad treaty network may be relevant if the structure meets PPT, LOB and anti-avoidance requirements. Treaty routing without real substance and a business purpose is not a viable strategy.

LIMITATIONS AND RISKS

Where Limitations and Risks Usually Arise

The Netherlands is a mature and recognised jurisdiction with a serious legal system. But this does not mean it is suitable for every model. Several areas require careful assessment.

The Netherlands Is Not a Universal Answer for Treaty Structures

The broad DTA network attracts attention, but BEPS, PPT, LOB tests and anti-avoidance provisions in Dutch treaties require the structure to have a genuine business purpose. Using the Netherlands solely as a transit treaty structure without real substance is a vulnerable position.

Holding Structures without Real Substance Are Vulnerable

A Dutch holding company requires real substance: directors with authority, board meetings in the Netherlands and local decision-making. A nominee director and registered address without operational content are not sufficient for either treaty access or banking onboarding.

Participation Exemption and Treaty Benefits Must Match the Real Facts

The participation exemption is a valuable tool, but it is conditional. Subject-to-tax requirements, anti-abuse provisions and substance conditions are applied more strictly each year. Case-specific analysis is essential.

VAT and EU Trade Can Add Complexity

Dutch VAT obligations are real and require corresponding operational readiness. For groups with EU-facing sales, VAT compliance requires a structured system. This is not an obstacle, but it is an operationally costly option.

Local Governance and Documentation Matter More than Founders Usually Assume

Dutch corporate law requires proper corporate records, board resolutions and formal procedures. This is not merely a formality — the company’s tax residency, treaty eligibility and banking onboarding may depend on it.

A Dutch Company Must Have a Real Role in the Group, Not Merely a Formal One

If a Dutch company exists only on paper while real decisions are made in another jurisdiction, this creates a risk of residency being recognised elsewhere, as well as problems with treaty access and banking onboarding. Form without substance does not work.

MLI, PPT and Evolving Anti-abuse Practice

The Netherlands actively applies the MLI, the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and other anti-abuse provisions. A structure must withstand analysis of business purpose and economic substance. Formal compliance without real business logic is not enough.

PRACTICAL APPLICABILITY

For Which Models the Netherlands Is More Often Relevant

The Netherlands works well in specific scenarios — where the structure, functions and real business logic are aligned.

Holding Role in a Real Group Structure

Where a Dutch company performs a real holding function: managing participations, receiving dividends, IP holding — with real substance, a documented board and operational logic.

What to check: substance requirements, participation exemption conditions, WHT on dividends, treaty eligibility and management & control, CFC exposure for shareholders.

Operating Company with EU-facing Activity

Where a company genuinely operates in the EU, has EU customers, EU contracts and EU operational presence. In this case, a Dutch operating company is a logical and organic choice.

What to check: VAT registration and compliance, CIT obligations, employer duties, group structure alignment, PE-risk.

Service or Coordination Company with Real Functions

Coordination, treasury or intragroup service functions — where there are real people, real agreements on an arm's length basis and documented decision-making in the Netherlands.

What to check: transfer pricing documentation, substance, management & control, real vs nominal functions, banking expectations.

VAT-relevant Trading or Commercial Model

For EU-based trading, e-commerce or distribution with a Dutch operational centre — where VAT compliance, EU customs and logistics logic genuinely point to the Netherlands as a natural base.

What to check: VAT obligations, EU customs duties, warehouse / fulfilment risks, fixed establishment exposure, group tax coherence.

Structures Where Reputation, Governance and Treaty Relevance Matter Together

For certain investor-facing or regulated contexts where the Dutch jurisdiction adds real value through the legal system, banking access and international reputation.

What to check: specific regulator or investor requirements, substance, documentation trail, comparison with alternatives.

WHAT TO KNOW

What Is Important to Understand in Advance

The Netherlands is not a universal answer for every group structure. Its treaty network is impressive, but treaty benefits work only where real conditions are met — substance, business purpose and anti-abuse tests.
Treaty access does not replace real substance and governance. A Dutch company without real functions, people and documentation is a vulnerable position for both tax authorities and banks.
VAT, tax, banking and operational logic must be aligned. Choosing a jurisdiction based on one factor is not enough. All elements of the structure must work as a single system.
The choice of jurisdiction must match real functions and risks. The Netherlands works where there are real operations, real people and real business logic — not only legal form.
FAQ

Briefly, to the Point

When is the Netherlands relevant for an international structure?

The Netherlands should be considered not as a universal solution, but as an element of a structure — for a group of companies or a single-company model where management, functions and client logic genuinely align with this jurisdiction. Relevance is determined by real presence and consistency with the wider model, not by a rate or speed of registration in isolation.

What is assessed first when choosing the Netherlands?

The first assessment covers operating presence, VAT, people footprint, governance, banking readiness and tax reality. It is important to determine whether the stated role of the Netherlands in the structure matches the actual activity: where the people, decisions and functions are located. The jurisdiction is not reviewed in isolation, but for coherence with the group and its banking and regulatory logic.

Where do limitations and risks most often arise in the Netherlands?

Risks arise where the structure in the Netherlands diverges from reality: presence only on paper, management in another country, or mismatch between functions and profit. This creates questions around PE-risk, tax residency and bank readability. Limitations emerge during review by a bank, tax authority or regulator if the model does not withstand economic reality.

Which models is the Netherlands best suited for?

The Netherlands is more often relevant for models where the role of the jurisdiction is supported by real presence and aligned with the wider structure. It may be an element of an international group or a single-company model, provided there is real management, governance and substance. Selection always starts from the logic of the whole model, not from choosing a country as the starting point.
