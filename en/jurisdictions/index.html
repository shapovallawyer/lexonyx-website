Jurisdictions for international business

A jurisdiction is not a tax rate or the speed of incorporation. It is a role in the structure, management reality, tax applicability, banking readability, substance and regulatory compatibility.

PRINCIPLE

Jurisdiction as an element of the structure, not as a standalone solution

We do not prepare country rankings and do not select a jurisdiction “by the rate”. The assessment starts with function: what role the country must play in the business model — for a group of companies or for a single-company business.

For a group of companies

We assess the jurisdiction as a place for a holding, operating, coordination or investor function.

For a single-company business

We look at tax residency, effective management, banking applicability and operational reality.

For a regulated model

We match the country with the licensing perimeter, governance requirements and regulator expectations.

SELECTION LOGIC

What roles a jurisdiction can play in a structure

The same country may be appropriate in one role and create risk in another.

Holding and coordination role

A country for ownership, investment logic, treaty use and group coordination.

Operating company

A jurisdiction where the business has real activity, employees, counterparties, VAT and banking history.

Founder model

Where the country is closely connected with personal residency, the place of management and actual control of the business.

Investor or premium model

Where reputation, depth of governance, banking strictness and external readability of the structure matter.

Regulated activity

Where the choice of country is determined by the licensing perimeter, governance requirements and the permissible operating model.

WHEN THIS IS NEEDED

When jurisdiction analysis is needed, rather than just a list of countries

There are situations where the choice of country is already part of a structural decision with tax, banking and operational consequences.

The structure already exists, but the selected country no longer matches the real functions, team or regulatory tasks.

Founders or key participants have relocated, creating a risk of changes in management, CFC exposure or permanent establishment.

Banking applicability is needed: the structure must be understandable and explainable to a bank or payment provider.

A licensing task arises: payment, crypto or other regulated activity requires the right jurisdiction.

The sales model, VAT or warehouse logic changes, and the current country creates new obligations or substance risk.

The business is scaling into the EU, United Kingdom, UAE or Switzerland and must withstand bank, investor or regulator review.

FAQ

Briefly, to the point

How does LEXONYX approach jurisdiction selection?

A jurisdiction is considered as an element of the structure, not as a standalone solution. It is not about the tax rate or the speed of incorporation, but about the role in the model: management reality, tax applicability, banking readability, substance and regulatory compatibility. The choice always follows the logic of the whole structure, not a ranking of countries.

Which jurisdictions do you work with?

We most often work with EU, UK and UAE jurisdictions: Cyprus, Estonia, Lithuania, Ireland, the Netherlands, Malta, Poland, the Czech Republic, as well as the United Kingdom, the UAE and Switzerland. Each is considered not as a universal solution, but as a possible role in the structure — depending on what the specific business model requires.

What roles can a jurisdiction play in a structure?

A jurisdiction can perform different roles: holding, operating, asset ownership, a management centre or a presence for a specific market. It is important that the selected role is supported by real substance and aligned with the rest of the structure. The same country can be both a suitable and an unsuitable element in different models.

How does jurisdiction analysis differ from a list of countries?

A list of countries answers the question “where is the rate lower”, while analysis answers the question “what role does the structure need and which jurisdiction can perform it”. Analysis considers presence, management, tax and banking logic in combination. This is why it provides a defensible solution for a specific model, not a universal recommendation from a top list.
