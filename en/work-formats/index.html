CORE FORMAT

Strategic Structural Audit

For situations where a coherent system for the international business model must be assembled, rather than answering a single technical question.

  • appropriate when several blocks are affected at once: group, tax, PE-risk, VAT, banking, regulation, governance
  • delivers a structural map, risk map and solution options
  • forms an explainable and defensible logic for implementation

Rapid Risk Review

A short format to determine whether there is a real structural risk, how urgent it is and whether a deeper audit is needed.

  • appropriate for an initial assessment of the situation
  • helps identify hidden nodes before the problem scales
  • often becomes the entry point into an audit if the issue is deeper than it first appeared

Support and advisory

FAQ

Briefly, to the point

What work formats does LEXONYX offer?

Work formats indicate the depth of engagement: rapid risk review — a quick diagnosis of active risk zones; strategic structural audit — a comprehensive analysis of the entire international model as a system; and support/advisory — ongoing external support for the structure. The format is determined by the type of task and by which elements of the system are affected.

How do I choose the appropriate format?

The format depends on the task. If the priority is to understand quickly whether risks are active, the relevant format is a rapid review. If the model needs to be reassembled or defended as a whole, the relevant format is a strategic audit. If the structure is already in place and an ongoing adviser is needed as changes occur, the relevant format is support. A rapid review often becomes the entry point into a deeper format.

How does a rapid risk review differ from a full audit?

A rapid review is a quick diagnosis: it identifies which risk zones are already active and whether further work is required. A strategic audit is a comprehensive analysis of the entire model within one perimeter: group structure, PE-risk, VAT, tax residency, CFC, banking readiness and governance. A rapid review answers “is there a problem”; an audit answers “how should the system be reassembled”.

Can I start with a smaller format and move to a larger one?

Yes, this is a typical path. A rapid risk review often works as an entry point: it shows which zones are lighting up and whether a full strategic audit is needed. After the audit, the model can move into support mode so that it remains supported through growth and change without restarting the project each time.
