SYSTEM OF EXPERTISE

Group structuring — the centre of the whole architecture

Tax, transfer pricing, VAT, PE, banking and regulation do not exist in isolation. Each element tests whether the legal construct matches the real business, its people, functions and cash flows.

Group structure → ownership, company roles, functions and cash flows
Tax residency and CFC → control, management and the owner's tax exposure
DTT and WHT → treaty benefits, beneficial ownership, MLI and PPT
Transfer pricing → profit allocation across functions, assets and risks
VAT models → sales, services, marketplaces, warehousing and fulfilment
PE-risk → people, authority, contracts and actual presence
Banking readiness → KYC/AML logic, SOF/SOW and business narrative
Regulatory fit → permissibility of the model and the licensing perimeter
Substance & governance → the reality of the model and a verifiable decision trail

We do not sell jurisdictions — we build a system.

WHEN CLIENTS COME TO US

When you need architecture, not a single consultation

The work begins both when designing a new model and when reviewing an existing structure — before launching flows, a transaction, growth or an external review.

Entering a new country

You need to define the new company's role, tax, VAT, team, contracting model and banking flows before operations begin.

Building a holding group

You need to connect a HoldCo and operating companies with investment logic, DTT, WHT, substance and governance.

Founder relocation

Personal tax residency changes, while business management, the team, IP or assets remain in other countries.

An international team

Employees, directors or a sales team work from different countries and create PE, payroll, TP or governance risks.

A bank asks for explanations

A query on ownership, UBO, SOF / SOW, counterparties or the business model cannot be closed with a single document.

A structure with no single logic

Several companies, accounts, contracts and advisers exist separately but do not add up to an explainable model.

An investor, deal or due diligence

You need to check ownership, IP, intra-group transactions, tax, substance and documents in advance.

IP or intra-group financing

Royalties, loans, management fees or profit allocation arise and require TP, WHT and beneficial-ownership analysis.

WHEN TO REVIEW THE STRUCTURE

Business changes faster than its legal architecture

Even an initially correct structure becomes vulnerable when owners, the team, markets, company functions or the movement of money change.

Tax residency has changed

A founder, director or key manager has moved and continues to run the group from a new country.

A new country of operations

Clients, employees, a warehouse, contractors, a PSP or a local company have been added.

Company functions have changed

A company set up as a service entity now effectively runs sales, IP, financing or key risks.

Intra-group payments have appeared

Dividends, interest, royalties and management fees require DTT, WHT, TP, beneficial-ownership and PPT analysis.

An investment or sale is planned

An investor or buyer will review ownership, IP, contracts, tax, substance and the decision history.

The business is approaching regulation

A new service, product or payment model may change the licensing perimeter and presence requirements.

TYPICAL SITUATIONS

What structural work looks like in practice

Banking onboarding
Problem

A group across several jurisdictions was refused account openings: the bank could not trace the ownership logic and the origin of funds.

Approach

We rebuilt the business story, aligned the flows with company roles and prepared explainable SOF/SOW documentation to the bank's requirements.

Outcome

An ownership-and-flow map, an aligned business narrative and a set of SOF/SOW evidence were prepared for the banking review.

PE-risk and the team
Problem

Team growth across several countries created permanent-establishment risk and questions about the owner's tax residency.

Approach

We matched functions, authority and people's presence to the corporate architecture, and set up governance so that it matched reality.

Outcome

A PE-risk map, a team authority matrix and a governance model aligned with the actual management of the business were prepared.

Entering a new market
Problem

When entering the EU, a standard "by-country scheme" ignored VAT logic, the regulatory perimeter and presence requirements.

Approach

We designed the structure around the specific operating model: company roles, the VAT perimeter, presence and the permissibility of operations.

Outcome

A target Structure Map, a VAT roadmap, a regulatory perimeter and a list of questions for local tax and legal advisers were produced.

Actual projects and details are confidential. The scenarios are generalised and anonymised. They illustrate the nature of the work and are not a promise of a particular outcome: decisions are made independently by banks, regulators and tax authorities.
